# Blood Components and Terminology

Title: Blood Components and Terminology
Topic: Terminology
Source: DonorConnect Knowledge Base

Human blood consists of several major components, each serving specific physiological functions in the human body:

1. Red Blood Cells (Erythrocytes):
   - Transport oxygen from the lungs to body tissues and carry carbon dioxide back to the lungs.
   - Used for trauma victims, surgical patients, and severe anemia treatments.
   - Storage life is up to 35-42 days under refrigeration.

2. Platelets (Thrombocytes):
   - Tiny cell fragments that help form blood clots and stop bleeding.
   - Critical for cancer patients undergoing chemotherapy, organ transplant recipients, and major surgery patients.
   - Storage life is short (typically 5 days at room temperature under constant agitation).

3. Plasma:
   - The liquid portion of blood (about 55% of total blood volume) containing proteins, clotting factors, and antibodies.
   - Used for severe burn victims, trauma care, and patients with clotting disorders.
   - Can be frozen and stored for up to one year (Fresh Frozen Plasma).

4. Blood Groups:
   - Main ABO blood groups are A, B, AB, and O, with Rh factor (Positive or Negative).
   - O Negative (O-) is considered the universal red blood cell donor because O- red blood cells can be transfused to patients of any blood group in emergency situations.
   - AB Positive (AB+) is considered the universal recipient for red blood cells.
