# When to Contact a Healthcare Professional

Title: When to Contact a Healthcare Professional
Topic: Medical Advice
Source: DonorConnect Knowledge Base

While blood donation is safe and well-tolerated by the vast majority of donors, certain symptoms after donation warrant prompt medical attention.

When to Seek Immediate Medical Care or Emergency Services:
- Severe chest pain, shortness of breath, or difficulty breathing.
- Fainting, loss of consciousness, or severe lightheadedness that does not improve after sitting or lying down.
- Heavy or persistent bleeding from the venipuncture site that does not stop after applying firm pressure for 10-15 minutes.
- Severe pain, numbness, tingling, or swelling radiating down the arm from the puncture site.
- Signs of systemic infection, such as high fever, chills, or persistent nausea within 24 to 48 hours post-donation.

Informational Disclaimer:
DonorConnect is an informational platform for matching potential donors with urgent blood requests. DonorConnect does NOT provide medical diagnosis, individual medical eligibility determinations, emergency treatment decisions, or clinical advice. If you or someone else is experiencing a medical emergency, call your local emergency number (such as 108 or 911) or visit the nearest hospital emergency room immediately. Always consult a qualified physician or blood bank medical officer for medical advice.
